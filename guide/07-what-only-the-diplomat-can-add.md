# 7. What only the diplomat can add

A mission of agents writes from the public record and a one-page profile. Everything outside those two is the
diplomat's, and it is most of what makes diplomacy work.

## What stays with people

- **The room.** Who spoke to whom before the meeting, which delegation was unusually quiet, which phrase was
  chosen with care. None of it is in a transcript.
- **Relationships.** Trust built over years, favours owed, the partner who needs a quiet abstention rather than a
  loud vote.
- **Instructions and politics at home.** What the capital wants, what it can accept, what it cannot say aloud.
- **Timing.** When to speak, when to wait, when a position should move before the record shows any reason to.
- **Judgement.** Whether a position that follows correctly from the profile is still the right one.
- **Responsibility.** A State's position is taken by people who answer for it.

## How the two fit together

Use the agents for what they do well and keep the rest:

- Let them cover the meetings no one can attend, and read the triage every morning.
- Use their reports as sourced first drafts; check the passages that matter before quoting them.
- Read their options memos as a checklist of alternatives, not as advice.
- Treat their decisions as a test of the profile: when the agents reach a position you would not take, ask
  whether the profile says what you mean.
- Use the record of positions over time to prepare a negotiation: what the State said before, and with whom it
  stood.

## Lines not to cross

- Do not feed them non-public material unless your rules allow the provider to see it.
- Do not present their output as the State's position.
- Do not let their fluency stand in for verification.

## Checklist

- [ ] We have written down what the agents will be used for, and what they will not.
- [ ] Every use of their output has a person who checks it against the record first.
- [ ] Nothing non-public goes in without explicit permission.
- [ ] Nothing they write is presented as the State's position without a person deciding it.
- [ ] We review, from time to time, whether the profile still says what we mean.
