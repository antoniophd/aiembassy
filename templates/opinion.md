# Opinion

What one desk adds to a full file. Written by each counsellor the triage assigns, other than the lead, after the
report is accepted and before the options memo. It does not summarise the meeting: the report has done that.

```
OPINION OF THE <desk> COUNSELLOR
<meeting> · <date> · on: <the decision point>

<The one thing this desk adds on the decision point, in its own field only, and what it means for the
State's interests. At most 250 words, in prose.>

Interests engaged: <I-ids>
Red lines engaged: <R-ids, or none>
Objection: <yes | no>
```

> Each desk keeps to its field. Legal: the competence of the body, the legal value of the text, the applicable
> law, the legal arguments made. Economic: economic, environmental and budgetary consequences. Political:
> positions, groups, votes and the balance of support.
>
> The agents read the debate, not the draft texts. An opinion reasons from what was said and does not speculate
> about wording it has not seen; a legal opinion is published under the line "Based on the debate; the draft
> text was not reviewed."

## Invented example

> OPINION OF THE LEGAL COUNSELLOR
> Committee on Shared Waters · on: the text asking basin States to share river data, to be adopted
>
> The text is a recommendation: it asks, it does not bind. Arvenia's argument that it creates a duty to share
> data within thirty days therefore overstates it, and Belmora's reply that the request leaves each State its
> own timetable is closer to the debate. For the State, supporting the text costs no legal commitment and keeps
> it on the side of agreed rules, its first interest. No red line is touched.
>
> Interests engaged: I1 · Red lines engaged: none · Objection: no

## What it must carry

- One contribution, from the desk's own field, on the decision point.
- What it means for the State's interests, by their ids in the profile.
- A red line only if the triage listed it and the matter really touches it.
- An objection only when the desk would advise against going ahead; the file shows the opinion as an objection.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Summarising the meeting or repeating sentences of the report.
- Covering the ground of another opinion: two opinions that overlap are a sign of a vague brief. Check the
  overlap between opinions by code.
- Arguments from another desk's field.
- If the vote is over, advice on how to vote; write about what it means now and the next step.
