# Statement or explanation of vote

The words the State's representative speaks in the meeting. Written by the press counsellor only when the
decision takes the floor, and only from the decision: it conveys exactly the position decided, nothing more.
Nothing is delivered by the system; a person decides what is said in the room.

```
<STATEMENT | EXPLANATION OF VOTE>
<meeting> · <date>

<First person ("We …", "My delegation …"). What the State does and why, in public terms.
At most 200 words.>
```

> Checks, by code: the length; the first person; no internal term (the decision, the options, the memo,
> interests or red lines by their ids, the models or the process).

## Invented example

> EXPLANATION OF VOTE
> Committee on Shared Waters
>
> My delegation voted in favour of the text on shared river basins. Water that crosses borders needs rules that
> hold for everyone, and small countries depend on those rules more than anyone. The text asks basin States to
> share river data in time, so that floods are predicted earlier and harvests are protected. We support exactly
> that. We will vote the same way in the plenary, and we will keep working with all delegations to make the data
> flow in practice.

## What it must carry

- The position decided, with its vote, unchanged.
- The State's interests as it would say them in public.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Internal reasoning: the options, the memo, interests or red lines by their ids, or the internal process.
- Sentences copied from the decision.
- "Voted" for a text adopted without a vote: the State "joined the consensus on" it or "supported" it.
