# Press pack

What the mission says and publishes about a position, in one document with three parts. Written by the press
counsellor when the decision asks for a public line, only from the accepted documents of the file: the same
option and the same vote as the decision, no new names, figures or symbols. Nothing in it is released by the
system; a person decides what leaves the building.

```
## Stakeout remarks                      (first person, said aloud, 120–180 words)
<the main message, in one short sentence>
<what happened, in the State's words>
<why the State acted as it did, in public terms>
<what comes next>

Q: <the question the press is most likely to ask>     (≤ 30 words)
A: <a brief answer, on message>                        (≤ 60 words)

## Press release                         (third person, 200–300 words with headline and dateline)
**<Headline: what the State did>**
<City>, <day month year>

<What happened, in <the body, without the meeting number>: the texts, the result; for each text, a vote
(with the tally if there was one) or adoption without a vote.>

<The State's position and the reason, in public terms.> "<one or two sentences of the stakeout remarks, word for
word, that say why>," <head of mission> said.

<What comes next.>

## Social media post                     (≤ 280 characters, no platform, no emoji, ≤ 1 hashtag)
<one or two sentences> #<OneTag>
```

> Checks, by code: the length of each part; every name, figure and symbol appears in the accepted documents;
> option and vote equal the decision's, and no sentence has the State vote otherwise; "voted" only for a text
> put to a vote (a text adopted without a vote is "joined the consensus on" or "supported"); the quote is one or
> two sentences of the remarks, word for word, giving the reason, not the headline or the list of texts; the release
> has three paragraphs and names the body without the meeting number; no delegation in a sentence of blame or regret; no mention of
> the platform, models or the internal process. One retry with the reasons; then the file goes on without it.

## Invented example

Arvenia, Belmora, the Coastal Group and the "Committee on Shared Waters" do not exist.

### Stakeout remarks

My country voted in favour of the text on shared river basins and joined the consensus on drought early warning. Water that crosses borders needs rules that hold
for everyone. That is the heart of this text, and that is why we supported it. The Committee adopted it by 98
votes. We welcome that result. Small countries depend on rules more than anyone. When upstream and downstream
States share data, floods are predicted earlier and harvests are protected. This text asks for exactly that. We
listened to every delegation in the room. Arvenia and Belmora both spoke for early warning, and we share that
aim. The work is not over. The text now goes to the plenary. We will vote the same way there, and we will keep
working with all delegations to make the data flow in practice.

Q: Will the text change anything for countries downstream?
A: It sets a clear expectation that data is shared. Our task now is to make sure it is shared in time, every
season.

### Press release

**Shared river basins: the State backs the text**
<City>, 12 March 2031

The Committee on Shared Waters today adopted the text on shared river basins by 98 votes, and the text on
drought early warning without a vote. The State voted in favour of the first and joined the consensus on the
second. … (three short paragraphs, 200–300 words in all)

The State supported both texts because rivers that cross borders need rules that hold for everyone. "When
upstream and downstream States share data, floods are predicted earlier and harvests are protected," the head
of mission said.

Next comes the plenary vote, and the line holds.

### Social media post

Today the State voted in favour of the text on shared river basins. Water that crosses borders needs rules that
hold for everyone. #SharedWaters
