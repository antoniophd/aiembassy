# Cable to the capital

How the mission reports to the capital. Drafted by the lead counsellor and released by the head of mission,
written only from the accepted documents: it adds style, not facts. In a test setup the capital is not an actor:
it receives the cable and does not reply.

```
FROM:    Permanent Mission, <city>
TO:      Capital
DATE:    <date>
SUBJECT: <one line>

1. Summary
   <one paragraph with the gist>

2. <Short title>
   <one numbered paragraph per topic; synthesise, do not list delegations>

3. …

N. Assessment
   <full files: the position adopted, with whom the State coincides, the next step.
    reports: what to watch.>
```

About 900 words, one or two pages. Plain prose: no bullet points, no segment labels, no internal codes or model
names.

## Invented example (extract)

> SUBJECT: Committee on Shared Waters adopts river data text without a vote
>
> 1. Summary. The Committee adopted without a vote a text asking basin States to share river data within thirty
> days of collection. The Coastal Group carried the text; no delegation opposed it.
>
> 4. Assessment. The State supported the text and coincided with the Coastal Group. The next step is the
> assembly's plenary, where the text is expected to pass without a vote.

## What it must carry

- Only names, figures and symbols found in the accepted report.
- On full files, the decision's option and "stands with", unchanged.
- New sentences: a cable that repeats the report's sentences adds nothing.

## Mistakes to avoid

- New facts, or delegations without an entry of their own in the report.
- An assessment that softens or changes the decision.
- Segment labels like [E3]; the report carries the sources.
- Copying the example values of the prompt (use placeholders in prompts for that reason).
